The U.S. Lost 18% of Its New International Students, report says

By Aruzhan Zeinulla

New York, Nov. 25, 2026

Visa hurdles, immigration crackdown and a proposed $70,000 work-fee are pushing students elsewhere.

New international student enrollment at U.S. colleges fell 18% last fall, the second straight annual decline, according to the Open Doors report released earlier this month by the Institute of International Education (IIE).

Among schools reporting declines, major reasons are visa application process and travel restrictions. The Department of Homeland Security proposed a new rule requiring schools to pay a $70,000 fee for every student who participates in OPT, a program that lets graduates work in the U.S.

Graduate students remain the largest group of international students in the U.S., but there has been a 25% decline in the new enrollment.

“There are more countries actively investing in and recruiting international students. So as a result, this often means that there are more destination opportunities," said Mirka Martel, IIE's head of research.
